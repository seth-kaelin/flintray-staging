The FlintRay measurement standard

This is how we measure which companies AI assistants put in front of your buyers, and how we report what we find. Every FlintRay report follows it, so you can hold our work to it.

Version 1.0, published November 2026.

The test setup

Assistants
ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini and Perplexity. Every situation runs in all four.
Conversations
Three turns: the problem, the constraints, then which companies the buyer would contact. Every assistant gets the same words.
Where it runs
Every conversation runs in the assistant's own web app, in a fresh session.
The model
Every answer is tied to the assistant that gave it, with the exact model wherever it's shown.
Reading
A person reads every answer before it counts.
Dates
Every run is dated, and each report gives the dates its conversations ran.
Reruns
After a fix, the same situations run again in the same four assistants, word for word.

What one measurement is

One measurement is one buyer situation, asked as a three-turn conversation in ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini and Perplexity. Your name never appears in it, because what we want to know is whether the assistant finds you on its own.

  • The buyer situation

    Who the buyer is, what's happening, what they need and what rules options out. We write it from research into who buys from you, and a person reads each one before it runs and asks whether you could actually win it.

  • A run

    One pass through every situation in every assistant is a run. The free audit's 15 situations make 60 conversations.

  • Which model answered

    Claude and Gemini show the exact model, and we record it. ChatGPT and Perplexity don't say which model answered, so their answers show the assistant alone.

What counts as a mention

A company counts only when the assistant names it in its own words. We quote those words exactly, and if the quote isn't in the saved answer, the company doesn't count for that answer.

  • The final answer decides the outcome

    Companies can come up early in a conversation, so we track who's named at every turn. The outcome comes from the third answer, the one the buyer acts on.

  • A person reads every answer

    Anything unclear, like a name two companies share or a pick with conditions attached, is settled by that person before it counts.

  • Sources are what the assistant showed

    We record every source an assistant displayed with its answer and sort it by type: your site, a competitor's, a directory, a review site, a publisher or a forum. If an assistant read a page without showing it, we can't see that, and we don't guess.

  • Only complete conversations count

    A conversation that fails or gets cut short is reported as missing, and it's never filled in or estimated. Each report says how many conversations were planned and how many completed.

Five outcomes

When the buyer asks for names, every company in the answer gets one of five outcomes. Each one describes what happened in that single conversation.

  • Recommended

    The assistant tells the buyer to contact you.

  • Shortlisted

    You're on the list it suggests comparing.

  • Mentioned

    You're named, but the assistant doesn't put you forward.

  • Named negatively

    You're named, but with a warning to the buyer.

  • Not named

    You're absent from the answer.

"Put forward" means recommended or shortlisted, and the top pick is the first company an assistant recommends without conditions when the buyer asks for a shortlist. We report named, put forward, recommended and top pick separately, because being named and being chosen are different results.

How numbers are reported

Every number shows what it's out of, as in "put forward in 7 of 40", so you can see the base it stands on.

  • A base on every number we measure

    Our own figures never appear as a bare percentage. The count and the base come with them, and each one says which runs it comes from and when they ran.

  • Each assistant on its own

    Assistants disagree with each other, so reports show each one separately. For one B2B services company, ChatGPT put it forward in 75 of 148 conversations and Perplexity in 25 of 148.

  • Sources with their base too

    Some assistants show sources far more often than others. For that same company, ChatGPT showed at least one source in 147 of 148 conversations and Gemini in 115 of 148, so every source figure says how many conversations had sources at all.

  • Correlations are called correlations

    When two things move together, we say it's a correlation, in that word. A source that keeps appearing beside a recommendation shows what the assistant drew on, and we call it a cause only once changing it moves the next run.

Reruns and verdicts

Each fix in the plan names the situations it should move. Once it ships, we run those same situations again in the same four assistants, and the fix gets one of three verdicts.

  • Moved

    The answers changed the way the fix was meant to change them.

  • No change yet

    The answers haven't moved. Some changes take more than one run to show up.

  • Worse

    The answers moved against you, and the report says so plainly.

Every verdict comes with the conversations from before and after, so you can read the change yourself.

Answers also vary from one run to the next on their own. Research published by SparkToro found less than a 1-in-100 chance that an assistant gives the same list of brands twice1, and researchers at the University of St. Gallen found about 65% of cited sources change from one day to the next2. So one situation flipping once proves nothing. We report a rate only with its base, and we call something a trend only once reruns back it up.

How to read the evidence on this site

Evidence on this site comes with a plain sentence that says where it came from. Here's what each kind means.

  • Measured

    From conversations we ran and graded ourselves, in the four assistants. It always comes with its runs, its dates and its base.

  • Anonymized

    Measured for a client we don't name. We remove anything that could identify them, such as their name, niche, location and buyers, and we never show a competitor's domain. The label says the number is ours and anonymized, and gives the runs and dates.

  • Fictional sample

    Built to show what a report looks like. Every company, site and conversation in it is invented, and it never goes into a measured figure. The line reads: "Illustrative sample. Companies and sources are fictional."

  • Public source

    Someone else's published figure. We cite the publisher and the date, and where the publisher sells something the figure supports, we say that too.

What a run can't tell you

A measurement is only as useful as its limits are clear, and these are ours.

  • A run is a sample

    Answers change as models, apps and the web change. A run describes what happened on its dates, and trends need repeated runs.

  • Situations are our model of your buyers

    They come from research and review, and real buyers will phrase things their own way.

  • Sources stop at what's shown

    Some assistants show few sources or none, and a page an assistant read without showing it stays invisible to us.

  • Outcomes involve judgement

    Code checks every quote, but whether a quote recommends a company or only mentions it is a judgement call, so a person reads every answer and settles the unclear ones.

  • Four assistants cover part of AI search

    Assistants we don't run aren't in the figures, and each report says which ones ran.

  • Your pipeline is outside the run

    We measure what assistants tell buyers. We don't see your traffic, pipeline or deals unless you share them.
