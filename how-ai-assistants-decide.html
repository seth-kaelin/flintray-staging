How AI assistants decide which companies to recommend

When a buyer asks an AI assistant who to call, the answer names three or four companies. Those names get the first conversation. This is what we see deciding that list, from running buyers' real questions through ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini and Perplexity, and what a company can change.

Updated 2 November 2026.

The answer is built from the buyer's situation

Buyers don't type a keyword. They describe who they are and what they need, then add what rules options out: location, budget, timing, compliance, language. By the time they ask for names, the assistant is choosing among companies that appear to fit all of it.

A company that states those facts plainly is easy to fit. Where you operate, who you serve, what you cost and what you're certified for should be written down in words an assistant can quote, not left for a sales call.

Assistants read pages before they answer

When an assistant searches the web, it reads a handful of pages and builds its shortlist from them. Many answers show those pages as sources. In our audits, the sources that decide a shortlist are usually a mix of the companies' own pages, directories, review sites, comparison articles and industry publishers.

That means a shortlist is often assembled from your competitors' pages. If their pages answer the buyer's exact question and yours don't, they get named.

A specific page beats a general one

The most consistent pattern across our audits: the company that wins a situation usually has a page written for exactly that situation. A buyer asking about a particular program, a particular kind of customer or a particular requirement gets sent to the company that has a page about it, even when a competitor offers the same thing and only mentions it on a general services page.

The reverse also holds. When an assistant does read a company's own page for a situation, it often recommends that company.

Contradictions cost you

Assistants quote pages closely. When two of your pages disagree, say a customer count that differs between pages, or a certification described as complete on one page and in progress on another, the assistant repeats one of them or leaves you out to be safe. We have found contradictions like this in every audit so far. They are usually the fastest fix on the list.

Third-party pages carry weight

Directories, review sites and "best of" comparison articles show up repeatedly as sources. If your competitors are listed there and you aren't, the assistant builds its shortlist from a list you're not on. Being present and accurate on the pages assistants already read matters as much as your own site.

Every assistant answers differently

The same company, asked about with the same questions, gets different results in each assistant. For one client, ChatGPT recommended the company in 75 of 148 conversations; Perplexity did in 25 of 148. Each assistant reads different sources and weighs them differently, so a fix that moves one may not move another. Checking a single assistant gives a misleading picture.

Smaller companies can win specific situations

In our audits, the biggest names took most of the broad questions, while a smaller company did far better on narrower ones, such as buyers in the area it is best known in. Narrow situations are where a focused page makes the most difference, and where an audit tends to find the quickest gains.

What to do about it

  • Find out where you stand first. Run your buyers' real questions, as full conversations, through all four assistants, and note who gets named instead of you.
  • Write a page for each situation you're losing. Answer the buyer's exact question, with the facts that rule competitors in or out.
  • Make your pages agree with each other. One number, one status, used everywhere.
  • Get listed where the shortlists are built. Start with the directories and comparison pages the assistants already cite in your market.
  • Measure again. Rerun the same questions after each change. It is the only way to know which change moved the answer.

How we know

These observations come from our own audits. Each one runs a company's buyer situations as three-turn conversations (the problem, the constraints, then "which companies would you contact?") in ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini and Perplexity, in the same apps buyers use. We record the model behind every answer and the pages it read. A company counts as recommended only when the assistant's own words name it. Assistants change often, so we rerun on a schedule rather than trust a single snapshot. Client details are kept anonymous.
