How AI assistants decide which companies to recommend

When a buyer asks an AI assistant who to call, the answer is a short list of companies. Those names get the first conversation. This is what we see deciding that list, from running buyers' real questions through ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini and Perplexity, and what a company can change.

Updated 2 November 2026.

The answer is built from the buyer's situation

A real buying question is rarely a keyword. In the conversations we run, the buyer describes who they are and what they need, then adds what rules options out, such as location, budget or compliance. By the time they ask for names, the assistant is choosing among companies that appear to fit all of it.

A company that states those facts plainly is easy to fit. Where you operate, who you serve, what you cost and what you're certified for should be written down in words an assistant can quote, not left for a sales call.

Assistants read pages before they answer

In the audits we reviewed, assistants that searched the web showed the pages they read as sources, and their shortlists drew on those pages. The sources were a mix of the companies' own pages, directories, review sites, comparison articles and industry publishers.

Competitors' own pages were among those sources. Where a competitor's page answered the buyer's exact question and the company's page didn't, the competitor was the one named.

A specific page beats a general one

A pattern we saw in our audits: the company that won a situation had a page written for exactly that situation. A buyer asking about a particular program or requirement was sent to the company with a page about it, even when a competitor offered the same thing and only mentioned it on a general services page.

The reverse held too. When an assistant did read a company's own page for a situation, it recommended that company more often than not.

Contradictions cost you

Assistants quote pages closely. When two of your pages disagree, say a customer count that differs between pages, or a certification described as complete on one page and in progress on another, the assistant may repeat the wrong one, or leave you out. We found contradictions like this in the audits we reviewed, and they were among the quickest fixes.

Third-party pages carry weight

Directories, review sites and "best of" comparison articles were among the sources in those audits. If your competitors are listed there and you aren't, the assistant builds its shortlist from a list you're not on. Being present and accurate on the pages assistants already read can matter alongside your own site.

Every assistant answers differently

The same company, asked about with the same questions, gets different results in each assistant. For one client, ChatGPT recommended the company in 75 of 148 conversations; Perplexity did in 25 of 148. Results can differ between assistants, so a fix that moves one may not move another. Check all of them.

Smaller companies can win specific situations

In the audits we reviewed, the biggest names took most of the broad questions, while a smaller company did far better on narrower ones, such as buyers in the area it is best known in.

What to do about it

  • Find out where you stand first. Run your buyers' real questions, as full conversations, through all four assistants, and note who gets named instead of you.
  • Write a page for each situation you're losing. Answer the buyer's exact question, with the facts that rule competitors in or out.
  • Make your pages agree with each other. One number, one status, used everywhere.
  • Get listed where the shortlists are built. Start with the directories and comparison pages the assistants already cite in your market.
  • Measure again. Ask the same questions again after each change. It is the clearest way to know which change moved the answer.

How we know

These observations come from our own audits. Each one runs a company's buyer situations as three-turn conversations (the problem, the constraints, then "which companies would you contact?") in ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini and Perplexity, in the same apps buyers use. We record the model behind every answer and the pages it read. A company counts as recommended only when the assistant's own words name it. Assistants change often, so we rerun on a schedule rather than trust a single snapshot. Client details are kept anonymous.
