The shortlist forms before anyone visits your site

A buyer describes their situation to an AI assistant, adds the constraints that rule options out, then asks which companies to contact. The assistant answers with a few names and its reasons. Those companies get the first call. A company that isn't named never learns the deal existed, and nothing shows up in its analytics.

The companies named early in a conversation set the frame for the rest of it. So we measure the whole conversation, from the first question to the last.

What one answer looks like when you take it apart

Every conversation we run is saved in full. For each one, the report shows the situation, the exact questions, the assistant and the model the app showed, every company named and how, and the sources the app displayed.

Illustrative sample. Companies and sources are fictional.

SituationWritten from research into who buys. The client stays anonymous.

Hail-worn roof due for replacement, Aurora

The problemThe buyer's own framing, in the first turn.

Our Aurora home has the original 18-year-old shingles with some hail wear, so we're about due for a new roof. We're wondering whether to add solar at the same time. Bills are around $160 a month. Can you help me understand how to coordinate a roof replacement with a solar install, and whether doing both together is actually smart?

The constraintsWhat rules options out, in the second turn.

I'm worried about hail damaging panels, since we get bad storms here. I'm also confused about whether to use one company for both the roof and the solar or two separate ones, and I've heard horror stories about voided warranties. Our total budget across both projects is maybe $45,000. What should I compare, and how do I protect myself on warranties and insurance?

The askThe third turn. Its answer is the one we grade.

Thanks. Which companies around Aurora should I contact, both roofers that also do solar and dedicated solar installers? What should I ask each about hail ratings on panels, who's responsible if the roof leaks around the mounts, and how they'd handle removing and reinstalling panels if we need roof repairs later?

ModelRecorded from the app at the time of the answer, because the same assistant answers differently on different models.

Measured the way buyers actually ask

  • Buyer situation

    A real buyer type with a real problem, a budget, a deadline and constraints. Reviewed by someone who asks whether the client could actually win it.

  • Three turns

    The problem, then the constraints, then "which companies would you contact?" The third answer is the one we grade.

  • Four assistants

    ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini and Perplexity, in the apps buyers use, each in a fresh chat with no history. The model behind every answer is recorded.

  • Classification

    Recommended, shortlisted, mentioned or not named. Every call is backed by an exact quote.

  • Sources

    Every cited source, classified: your site, a competitor's, a directory, a review site, a publisher or a forum.

  • Fixes

    Specific pages, comparisons and listings, each with an owner.

  • Rerun

    The same situations, run again. Each fix is marked moved, no change yet, or worse.

How it works, in full

Each assistant gives a different answer

Each assistant reads different pages, so each one needs its own fix. A single blended score would hide this.

  • Named in 205 of 592 conversations, recommended in 191, and named first in 30.

    The same B2B services company and the same two runs, October 2026.

    ChatGPT named it first in 22 of those 30. Gemini never named it first in 148. Being mentioned, being recommended and being named first are different results.

  • Put forward in 7 of 14 conversations in its home state, and 2 of 39 everywhere else.

    A company that serves all 50 states, anonymized. One audit, November 2026.

    Assistants knew it locally and sent buyers everywhere else to competitors with a page for each situation.

  • Named in none of 56 conversations. The category leader was put forward in 39.

    A software company, anonymized. One audit, November 2026.

    Shortlists were built largely from competitors' own pages. None of the 56 cited the company's site.
