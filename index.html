An AI recommendation is word of mouth

When a buyer asks ChatGPT which companies to call, the answer lands the way a tip from a trusted colleague does. It gives a few names and a reason for each, and the one named first carries the most weight. A company that never gets named doesn't learn the deal existed, and nothing shows up in its analytics.

The decision starts forming in the first question

Buyers usually describe the problem first, then add what rules options out, and only ask for names at the end. By then the assistant may already have named a few companies. So we measure the whole conversation, every turn, and note who gets named at each step.

Across 712 buyer conversations in four AI assistants, a company that came up in the buyer's first questions and made the final shortlist was the top pick 1.7 times as often as one that only appeared at the shortlist, 113 of 888 against 241 of 3,144. It's a correlation, so we use the next run to check what actually moved.

What one conversation looks like when you take it apart

Every conversation we run is saved in full. For each one, the report shows the situation, each turn the buyer typed, the assistant and the model that answered, every company named and how, and the sources the answer cited.

Leaving spreadsheets and a consultant, Canada

The problemThe buyer's own framing, in the first turn.

I'm the operations lead at a 140-person analytics company. For two years we've maintained SOC 2 with a part-time consultant and a giant spreadsheet. The consultant is retiring next quarter, and honestly the spreadsheet only works because she understood it. Our next audit window opens in six months. I need to understand what my options are for replacing this setup before it collapses.

The constraintsWhat rules options out, in the second turn.

I'm not technical, and our engineers resent every compliance request. I'm afraid of losing history and evidence during a switch, or picking something that needs a full-time admin we don't have. Leadership wants costs roughly flat compared to the consultant. What should I compare between software, managed services and finding another consultant, and how do I judge migration risk?

The askThe third turn, where the buyer asks for names. We note who was named at every turn before it.

Thanks. Which specific compliance platforms or managed services should I contact for a company moving off spreadsheets with an audit six months out? What should I ask about migrating existing policies and evidence, how much onboarding help they give someone non-technical, and how they keep engineering requests to a minimum? I'd also like to know whether to keep our current auditor or reconsider.
