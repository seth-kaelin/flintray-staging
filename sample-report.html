The buyer's first message

I'm the cofounder of a 70-person logistics software company, and a large retailer told us they won't sign our biggest contract ever without a SOC 2 report. Procurement wants something by the end of Q1. We have zero formal compliance today, just a few security habits and a shared drive of half-written policies. I don't really understand what SOC 2 involves or what my options are for getting there. Can you walk me through how companies like ours usually approach this?

The assistant's answer

What SOC 2 is

SOC 2 is an attestation report from an independent CPA firm. It shows that your security controls are designed well (Type I) or that they operated well over time (Type II). Most buyers accept the Security trust criterion on its own for a first report.

Your realistic path

- Scope: pick the product, systems and data the retailer cares about.

- Gap assessment: compare your current habits against the SOC 2 criteria.

- Remediate: finish policies, enforce access reviews and logging, formalize onboarding and offboarding, and review key vendors.

- Type I: a point-in-time report, usually achievable in about three to four months from a standing start.

- Type II: covers an observation window of 3 to 12 months, so it usually comes later.

The common approaches

- Do it yourself with templates and spreadsheets.

- Use compliance-automation software plus a separate auditor.

- Pay a managed service to run most of the program.

With an end-of-Q1 deadline, most companies aim for Type I first. They then ask the buyer to accept a written Type II commitment.

The buyer's second message

Some constraints. Our engineering team is eight people and I can't pull more than one of them off product work. I'd rather not spend more than the deal is worth in year one. I'm also worried about buying a tool that promises automation but still leaves us doing everything by hand. We use a big cloud provider, an identity provider and a ticketing tool. What should I actually compare when looking at software, services and audit firms so I don't get burned?

The assistant's answer

What to compare in software

- Integrations: look for native connections to your cloud provider, identity provider and ticketing tool. They should run automated tests, not just accept file uploads.

- Evidence automation: ask what share of controls is monitored continuously and what share still needs manual screenshots.

- Policy templates and onboarding support.

What to compare in services

- Who writes the policies and closes the gaps.

- How many hours they expect from your engineer each week.

What to compare in audit firms

- How many SOC 2 audits they do for companies your size.

- Type I lead time and fixed fees.

- Whether they accept evidence directly from your platform.

Budget check

- Add up software, auditor, penetration test and internal time. Year-one totals often range from low five figures to low six figures.

Red flags

- Demos built on sample data.

- Vague 'automated' claims.

- No named owner during onboarding.

Before you sign, ask for a live demo connected to a sandbox of your own cloud account.
